Before you fall in love with a plan, take 60 seconds to confirm your device is eSIM-ready — it'll save you a headache later. Most modern smartphones launched after 2018 support eSIM, but there are a few exceptions worth knowing about. This page walks you through what to look for on iPhones, Android devices, and beyond.

## Is Your Device Compatible?

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="iPhone">
    Apple was an early champion of eSIM technology, so most recent iPhones are good to go. Here's a quick reference:

    **eSIM-compatible iPhone models:**

    - iPhone XS and XS Max
    - iPhone XR
    - iPhone 11, 11 Pro, and 11 Pro Max
    - iPhone 12 series (Mini, standard, Pro, Pro Max)
    - iPhone 13 series (Mini, standard, Pro, Pro Max)
    - iPhone 14 series (standard, Plus, Pro, Pro Max)
    - iPhone 15 series (standard, Plus, Pro, Pro Max)
    - iPhone SE (2nd generation and later)

    <Info>
      iPhone 14 models sold in the United States are **eSIM-only** — they don't have a physical SIM tray at all. If you have one of these, you're already living in the future. 🎉
    </Info>

    **Quick tip:** To confirm eSIM support on your iPhone, go to **Settings → General → About** and scroll down. If you see an **Available SIM** or **eSIM** section, you're all set.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Android">
    eSIM support on Android varies more by brand and model, but the good news is that flagship and mid-range phones from the past few years almost always include it.

    **Popular eSIM-compatible Android devices:**

    - **Samsung:** Galaxy S20, S21, S22, S23, S24 (and all Plus/Ultra variants), Galaxy Z Fold and Flip series, Galaxy Note 20 series
    - **Google Pixel:** Pixel 3 and later (Pixel 3a, 4, 4a, 5, 6, 7, 8 series)
    - **Motorola:** Razr series, Edge series (select models)
    - **OnePlus:** Select models from the 9 series onward (region-dependent)
    - **Huawei:** P40 series and later (eSIM availability varies by region)
    - **Oppo / Find X series:** Select recent models

    **Quick tip:** On most Android phones, go to **Settings → Connections → SIM Manager** (or **Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs** on Pixel devices). If you see an option to add an eSIM or download a SIM, you're compatible.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Other Devices">
    It's not just phones — eSIM technology has spread to tablets and laptops too.

    **Tablets:**

    - **iPad Pro** (3rd generation and later), **iPad Air** (4th generation and later), and **iPad Mini** (5th generation and later) all support eSIM.
    - Many **Samsung Galaxy Tab** models support eSIM as well.

    **Laptops:**

    - Some Windows laptops (like the **Microsoft Surface Pro** LTE and newer models) and certain **Lenovo ThinkPad** and **HP EliteBook** models support eSIM for mobile data.

    <Info>
      eBanano plans are optimized and tested for **smartphones**. While you may be able to install an eSIM on a compatible tablet or laptop, we can't guarantee full functionality on non-phone devices. We recommend using eBanano on a smartphone and sharing your hotspot if you need data on other devices.
    </Info>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## A Word of Caution About Carrier-Locked Devices

<Warning>
  Even if your phone model normally supports eSIM, a **carrier-locked device** may block you from installing a third-party eSIM like eBanano. This is common with phones purchased directly from a carrier on a contract. Contact your carrier to check your lock status — or use your device's Settings to see if unlocking is available.
</Warning>

## How to Check If Your Device Is eSIM-Ready

Not sure where to look? Follow these steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your phone's Settings">
    On iPhone, tap the **Settings** app. On Android, tap **Settings** — it may look like a gear icon in your app drawer or notification shade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigate to SIM or Network settings">
    On **iPhone**: go to **Settings → General → About** and look for "Available SIM" near the bottom of the list.

    On **Android**: go to **Settings → Connections → SIM Manager** (Samsung) or **Settings → Network & Internet → SIMs** (Google Pixel and others). Look for an "Add eSIM" or "Download SIM" option.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look for eSIM or Add eSIM options">
    If you see an option to **Add eSIM**, **Download a SIM**, or similar wording — congratulations, your device is eSIM-ready! If you don't see any such option, your device may not support eSIM, or it may be carrier-locked.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Frequently Asked Questions

<Accordion title="My phone supports eSIM but it's not working — why?">
  A few things could be going on. First, check whether your device is **carrier-locked** (see the warning above). Second, make sure you haven't already reached your device's eSIM storage limit — some phones cap the number of stored eSIM profiles. Finally, double-check that you scanned the QR code correctly and that your phone's software is up to date. If you're still stuck, our support team is happy to help.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I use eBanano on a tablet?">
  Possibly! Some tablets (like newer iPad Pro and Samsung Galaxy Tab models) do support eSIM. However, eBanano is designed and optimised for smartphones, so we can't officially guarantee compatibility on tablets. A handy workaround: install eBanano on your phone and use the **mobile hotspot** feature to share the connection with your tablet.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What does 'carrier locked' mean?">
  When you buy a phone directly from a carrier — especially on a contract or instalment plan — the carrier sometimes "locks" the device so it only works with their network. This can prevent you from adding third-party eSIMs. Once your contract is fulfilled (or sometimes for a fee), you can usually request an unlock. Contact your carrier to find out your device's lock status and how to unlock it.
</Accordion>
